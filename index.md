# About Me

<img src="https://clydepsychology.github.io/clyde.jpg" class="floatpic" alt="Jincheng (Clyde) Wu">

Hi, I’m **Jincheng (Clyde) Wu**, a second-year master’s student in [**Interdisciplinary Studies in Human Development**](https://www.gse.upenn.edu/academics/interdisciplinary-studies-human-development-msed) at the University of Pennsylvania, advised by [Dr. Xinyin Chen](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=en&user=3tsgS14AAAAJ).

My research interests broadly center on **children’s and adolescents’ socio-emotional development**. I am interested in how **individual characteristics**, such as temperament, social cognition, and resilience, interact with **family, school, and cultural contexts** to shape young people’s social experiences and adjustment. Through this work, I hope to better understand how relationships and environments can support development across childhood and adolescence.

My work combines vignette-based tasks, peer nominations, questionnaires, and qualitative interviews. I also develop LLM-assisted programs to facilitate the coding and analysis of naturalistic language data. Through my research and experiences in youth mentoring and socioemotional learning, I hope to inform school- and family-based support for children and adolescents.

I received my B.S. in Applied Psychology from Zhejiang University of Technology, where I worked with [Dr. Yulong Tang](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Yulong-Tang-4) and [Dr. Shuyi Zhai](https://scholar.google.com/citations?hl=zh-CN&user=1U_lN9YAAAAJ). In Spring 2024, I was a visiting student at the University of California, Berkeley. My previous research experience also includes work with the [Family and Culture Lab at UC Berkeley](https://zhoulab.berkeley.edu/) and the [Life-span Development Laboratory at Stanford University](https://lifespan.stanford.edu/).

**PhD applications:** I am actively seeking potential PhD advisors and programs that align with my research interests for **Fall 2027 entry**, particularly in School Psychology, Developmental Psychology, and Human Development and Family Studies. I would be grateful for the opportunity to learn from and work with faculty whose research focuses on temperament, peer relationships, and school adjustment.

## Selected Research

### Shyness and Peer Relationships in School

How do children evaluate shy peers, and when do their preferences change? My research examines peer preferences across developmental stages and situations with different social demands. Related projects investigate how children’s own shyness, friendship characteristics, and perceptions of peer networks relate to school adjustment. This work includes several conference presentations and manuscripts under review or in preparation.

### Culture, Parenting, and Socio-emotional Development

How do cultural contexts and historical changes shape beliefs about children and their development? How do these beliefs, in turn, inform parenting and socialization practices and relate to children’s adjustment? As a research assistant in Dr. Xinyin Chen’s lab, I have contributed to projects examining children’s temperament, social behavior, peer relationships and leadership, school performance, and parenting within cultural contexts. These projects also address culturally relevant concepts and practices, including care-based power-assertive parenting and cultural values (self- and group-orientation). My contributions draw on multiple methods, including behavioral observations during free-play sessions, peer nominations, and questionnaires.

### Adolescent Stress and Support

How do adolescents experience stress, and what support do they find helpful? I contribute to mixed-methods research on Chinese American adolescents’ academic stress and support from peers, families, and schools. In a related area of applied work, I contribute to qualitative research and activity design for a school-based mentoring project using the Possibility Mentoring Framework.

### Social Cognition and LLM-Assisted Corpus Analysis

My research experience also includes studies of children’s social cognition, including exploratory behavior and selective trust in gossip. I develop LLM-assisted programs to support the coding and analysis of naturalistic language data. In a project on young children’s gossip, I evaluated a coding framework combining LLM screening with human review. A manuscript from this work is under review.
